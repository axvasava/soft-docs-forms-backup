What Is a Campus Security Authority (CSA)?

Under the Jeanne Clery Disclosure of Campus Security Policy and Campus Crime Statistics Act (the Clery Act), certain individuals who have significant responsibility for student and campus activities are designated as Campus Security Authorities (CSAs).

You are considered a CSA if you:

  • Are a camp director, program supervisor, or lead instructor working with students on our campus
  • Have authority to take action in response to student misconduct or emergencies
  • Serve as a point of contact that students would naturally approach to report a crime or emergency

As a CSA, you are NOT a confidential resource. Your obligation is to report, not to investigate.

Your Reporting Obligation

If a student discloses to you, or you witness or have reasonable cause to believe that any of the following Clery Act crimes have occurred, you are required to report it to our institution as soon as possible:

Violent Crimes

  • Murder / Non-Negligent Manslaughter
  • Sexual Assault (Rape, Fondling, Incest, Statutory Rape)
  • Domestic Violence
  • Dating Violence
  • Stalking
  • Robbery
  • Aggravated Assault

Property & Other Crimes

  • Burglary
  • Motor Vehicle Theft
  • Arson
  • Hate Crimes (any offense motivated by bias)
  • Arrests or Referrals: Liquor, Drug, or Weapons Law Violations

Important: These crimes must be reported regardless of whether the victim wishes to report to law enforcement. Your job is to report to us -- we will handle the response from there.

When & How to Report

When: Report as promptly as possible -- ideally within 24 hours of learning of an incident.

How to Report:

  • For emergencies or immediate danger, call 911 first, then notify our office
  • You may also contact Campus Public Safety / Campus Police directly
  • Contact our designated CSA/Clery Coordinator directly (contact info below)

What to Include in Your Report: Approximate date, time, and location of the incident; a brief description of what occurred; whether anyone was injured; and your contact information. You do NOT need to include the victim's name if they wish to remain anonymous.

What You Should NOT Do

  • Do NOT investigate the incident yourself or confront a suspected offender
  • Do NOT promise confidentiality to a victim -- you are not a confidential resource
  • Do NOT discourage a victim from reporting to law enforcement
  • Do NOT delay reporting while waiting to see if a victim wants to proceed
  • Do NOT share details of an incident beyond what is required for reporting purposes

If a student discloses an incident to you in confidence, gently let them know that you are required to report the information to the institution, but that you will support them through the process.

Protecting Victims & Maintaining Good Faith

The Clery Act protects reporters acting in good faith. As long as you report what you reasonably believe to be true, you will not face retaliation or liability for making a report.

Victims have rights and our institution has resources to assist them. When receiving a disclosure, you may provide the following reassurance:

  • "I'm here to support you."
  • "I do need to share this information with our institution's safety office, but they are here to help you."
  • "You have options, and I'll make sure you get connected with the right support."
